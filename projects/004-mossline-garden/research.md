# Day 04 research — Lusaka landscape and garden design

**Research date:** 18 November 2026  
**Project:** MOSS / LINE Garden Studio (fictional)  
**Market studied:** Lusaka, Zambia  
**Niche:** Residential and small-commercial landscape design, garden installation and ongoing care

## 1. Market snapshot

The local category spans several adjacent offers: garden design, landscaping installation, plant supply, lawn and garden maintenance, irrigation, outdoor living features and corporate greenery. A small studio can differentiate through a clearer design process, a more useful planting plan and a transparent handover/care offer rather than presenting itself as a general gardening contractor.

### Customer groups

- Homeowners planning a new garden or renovating an existing one.
- Owners of compact courtyards and townhouse gardens.
- Boutique hotels, guesthouses, restaurants and event venues.
- Offices, retail sites and commercial property managers.
- Developers or property managers seeking a consistent landscape concept and maintenance scope.

These segments are a market-analysis framework inferred from the services and customer groups visible in the cited local providers; they are not a published market-size estimate.

## 2. Common services and buying motivations

| Service pattern | What a customer is buying | Website information that helps |
|---|---|---|
| Garden design | A plan before committing to installation | Deliverables, site-review process, design examples, scope boundaries |
| Plant selection and supply | Confidence about what will suit a space | Plant roles, mature size, light/water notes, availability caveat |
| Installation and hardscape | Coordinated physical transformation | Stages, inclusions/exclusions, materials, site access and sequencing |
| Irrigation coordination | A practical way to manage water distribution | Site assessment, specialist scope, maintenance and water assumptions |
| Garden maintenance | A cared-for space without managing every task | Visit frequency, seasonal task lists, reporting and exclusions |
| Corporate/outdoor spaces | A coherent environment for staff, guests or visitors | Project scope, access planning, maintenance responsibility and contact path |

Customers are likely to value visual confidence, a clear scope, practical maintenance expectations, transparent estimates, and confidence that the selected plants and materials suit the site. These are design hypotheses for the fictional business, not survey findings.

## 3. Local seasonality and design implications

Zambia Tourism describes three broad seasons:
- **November–April:** hot and rainy, with frequent showers and bright periods.
- **May–August:** cooler and dry.
- **August–November:** hot and dry.

The same source describes rainfall as varying by region and gives a Lusaka annual figure in its climate overview. Because conditions vary between years and properties, the site does not turn this into a rigid planting calendar. It uses the seasons as prompts for observing drainage, irrigation, heat exposure, soil cover and maintenance timing.

A real project needs a site visit and locally appropriate horticultural advice. Soil, slope, shade, drainage, water access, plant availability, mature size and maintenance capacity all affect selection.

## 4. Competitor and reference observations

These are observations of publicly available website content, not endorsements or independent verification of service quality.

- **Hortus Eden — https://hortuseden.com/**  
  Its public homepage describes garden design, landscaping, maintenance and corporate greenery for Lusaka properties. The service grouping and site-visit call to action demonstrate a clear route from a broad need to a consultation.

- **Sarah Gardens — https://sarahgardens.com/plants-services/**  
  Its public services page groups landscaping, garden maintenance and plant sales. The page lists design, plant selection, lawn care, pruning, soil preparation and plant-health work. The combination suggests that a plant library and service catalogue can be useful to customers comparing options.

- **Trion Estates — https://www.trionestates.com/landscape-design-management**  
  Its landscape page describes garden and flower beds, patios, pergolas, outdoor kitchens, lighting, water features, walkways, stonework and maintenance. It also outlines consultation, design, installation and scheduled care. This reinforces the value of explaining process and project stages.

- **Lawn Irrigation Technologies — https://lawnirrigationtech.com/**  
  Its public site describes irrigation design, supply, installation, soil/site assessment, water efficiency and maintenance. It is a useful reference for explaining that irrigation is a technical scope rather than simply adding sprinklers to a garden.

## 5. Useful website features

1. A clear service map: design, installation and seasonal care.
2. A process page explaining site review, concept, scope, installation and handover.
3. A planting-role library that teaches customers how a palette is composed.
4. Clearly labelled concept studies that show visual direction without pretending to be completed client projects.
5. A project-brief builder collecting property type, location, approximate area, priority and budget.
6. FAQs covering existing gardeners, courtyards, irrigation coordination and plant selection.
7. Explicit contact and privacy boundaries for a static demo.

## 6. Trust signals and safeguards

For a real business, useful trust signals would include named and verifiable staff, a real project portfolio with client permission, references, itemised scopes, clear payment and variation terms, insurance and relevant professional credentials where applicable, plant and material specifications, aftercare instructions and an actual service address.

The fictional site deliberately does **not** claim professional registrations, completed projects, staff qualifications, client reviews, warranties or live availability. Any real irrigation, electrical lighting, structural work or other specialist activity must be checked and delivered by suitably competent and authorised providers as required.

## Research limitations

This is desk research from public websites, not a customer survey, supplier audit, nursery stock check, site inspection or formal market-sizing exercise. Public service descriptions can change. Plant recommendations, local availability, prices, licensing and technical requirements must be verified before any real commercial use.
