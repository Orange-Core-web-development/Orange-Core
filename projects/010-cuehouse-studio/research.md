# Day 10 research — experiential event production & corporate brand activations in Zambia

**Research date:** 24 November 2026  
**Scope:** Lusaka-led corporate events, conferences, product launches, exhibitions and brand activations.  
**Important:** The company and portfolio work described on the website are fictional. Market observations are sourced; the brand, service menu, sample projects and price assumptions are design assumptions.

## Niche and customer types

Experiential event producers coordinate the creative idea and the operational systems that turn a brand, business or institution's objective into an in-person or hybrid experience. Buyers include marketing teams, communications teams, HR and people teams, executive offices, associations, exhibitors, agencies and public-interest organisations. In Zambia, local service pages describe corporate functions, launches, conferences, exhibitions, activations, production, registration, logistics and attendee coordination as related service lines.

## Common services and buying motivations

- **Event strategy & creative concept:** translate a brief, audience and desired outcome into a format, narrative and look.
- **Production & staging:** stage, lighting, sound, screens, scenic elements, print and signage.
- **Brand activation:** pop-ups, interactive experiences, roadshows and sampling/engagement formats.
- **Conference operations:** registration, programme flow, speaker liaison, guest movement, supplier coordination and on-site management.
- **Content capture / post-event:** photography, video, recap assets and a post-event report.

Buyers are seeking a credible, accountable partner; predictable delivery; clear budgets and scope; a strong audience experience; brand consistency; and fewer suppliers to coordinate. These motivations are strategic design assumptions informed by the service models documented in the cited sources—not survey findings about Zambian customers.

## Market conventions and trust signals

Local reference sites commonly show services, project imagery, contact/quote pathways and event types. B2B briefs often request event name/type, date, location, duration, attendee estimate, services required and a detailed brief. Strong trust signals include a named point of contact, clear process, realistic scope, transparent inclusions/exclusions, supplier contingency plans, case studies that can be verified, and safety/access information. A portfolio should not claim client logos, attendance numbers, awards or credentials without permission and evidence.

## Useful website features

- Service filters or clearly separated event types.
- Case-study cards that explain challenge, concept, production choices and outcome; mark fictional examples as concepts.
- A four-stage process from discovery to close-out.
- Event-brief builder that captures date, venue, audience, objectives, budget band and required services.
- Accessible mobile navigation, clear calls to action, FAQs, privacy notes and a visible distinction between enquiry and confirmed booking.

These figures are not transferable to Zambia; the report is used only as a global B2B design reference for event measurement and repeatable event programmes. [Source](https://www.cvent.com/en/press-release/splashs-2025-outlook-events-88-marketers-identify-events-key-revenue-driver)

## Design assumptions for CUEHOUSE

CUEHOUSE is a fictional Lusaka studio for corporate brand experiences, product launches, conferences, internal culture events and pop-ups. It is intentionally not positioned as a wedding decorator. The visual identity uses warm paper, ink black, orange-red and acid-lime with grid layouts, stage-light graphics and punchy typography. Sample service pricing bands and fictional concept projects are illustrative only. No real client, performance, venue partnership, contact or testimonial is implied.

## Source verification notes

The cited local pages were opened and their service descriptions and enquiry structure checked on 24 November 2026. Pages can change; verify their current details before using them as competitive references in a client pitch. The Cvent survey's population is explicitly U.S. marketers and should not be generalized to Zambia.
