# Day 12 research — commercial refrigeration and cold-chain engineering in Zambia

**Checked:** 26 November 2026. **Scope:** Lusaka-led commercial refrigeration, with projects elsewhere in Zambia by arrangement. MIZI Coldworks is fictional; market observations are sourced, while the proposed brand, services, policies and case studies are design assumptions.

## Niche and customers
Commercial refrigeration contractors design, supply, install, commission, repair and maintain display chillers, walk-in cold rooms, freezer rooms, blast-chilling/freezing equipment and larger processing/cold-storage plants. Buyers include butcheries and meat processors; poultry, fish and dairy operators; farms and produce packhouses; supermarkets, wholesalers and beverage distributors; restaurants, hotels and caterers; and specialist pharmaceutical or healthcare operators.

## Common services and products
- Chilled cold rooms and walk-in chillers.
- Freezer rooms and frozen-stock storage.
- Blast chillers and blast freezers for process-led applications.
- Display cabinets, bottle coolers, island freezers and preparation refrigeration.
- Insulated panels, doors, refrigeration plant, components and controls.
- Installation, commissioning, fault diagnosis, repairs and plant upgrades.
- Preventive maintenance, service contracts, temperature monitoring and operator handover.

These service categories are visible in the published menus of Zambian contractors such as Drake & Gorham, Insulated Systems, Siams RB, Kaylite Zambia and Forty Below Refrigeration.

## Buying motivations and market conventions
Customers want to protect stock, reduce spoilage and downtime, keep operations open, manage running costs, meet product/process requirements and secure dependable local service. Public contractor websites commonly foreground a quote or site-survey CTA, list sectors served, show equipment categories and explain a sequence such as survey → design/sizing → installation/commissioning → service. Maintenance contracts and parts/support availability are recurring differentiators.

A useful project brief should ask about product type, target conditions from the product specification, typical and peak throughput, room dimensions, door cycles, loading/dispatch pattern, operating hours, available power and backup, access, drainage and future expansion. These are design recommendations, not a universal statutory checklist. A geometric room-volume calculator must not be represented as a refrigeration-capacity calculator.

## Trust signals and regulatory context
Useful trust signals include verifiable project photography with permission, named equipment and model details, checkable staff credentials, transparent scopes/exclusions, documented commissioning, manufacturer warranty terms, service history, temperature records, spare-parts support and realistic response commitments.

Zambia's Food Safety Act, 2019 is listed by the National Assembly's Parliamentary Budget Office as Act No. 7 of 2019 and provides a framework for protecting the public against food-related health hazards and fraud. ZCSA explains that compulsory standards contain mandatory specifications that products must meet before market supply. Refrigeration alone does not make a food operation compliant.

For medicines and vaccines, specialist requirements apply. WHO publishes temperature-monitoring device guidance; many vaccines are traditionally stored at 2°C–8°C, while controlled-temperature-chain exceptions are product-specific and separately defined. Do not apply vaccine requirements to ordinary food storage or claim healthcare capability without appropriate competence, equipment, monitoring and validation.
