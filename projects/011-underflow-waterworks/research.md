# Day 11 research report — Borehole drilling & groundwater systems in Zambia

**Research checked:** 25 November 2026  
**Portfolio concept:** UNDERFLOW Waterworks (fictional)  
**Niche:** Borehole drilling, groundwater investigation, pump installation, water storage, reticulation and aftercare  
**Proposed base:** Makeni, Lusaka; service area concept includes Lusaka Province and selected nearby districts subject to mobilisation and site assessment.

## 1. Niche overview
The niche is a specialist, project-based service rather than a simple equipment shop. A customer is buying a water source and a working system: site investigation, drilling, casing, development, pumping tests, pump selection, power, storage, distribution and documentation. A useful website should explain how these parts fit together and be honest about what cannot be promised before the ground is investigated.

Zambian groundwater work has a specific regulatory context. WARMA publishes requirements for drilling-company licensing and borehole registration. Its published guidance lists company and technical credentials, drilling equipment and pump-test equipment for drilling-company licence applications. Its borehole-registration guidance describes landowner consent and site information requirements; for domestic sites it asks for a sketch/site map covering a 30 m radius and showing boundaries, roads and distances from sanitation or other potential pollution sources. For non-domestic sites it lists a site-investigation report with planned location, existing boreholes within 250 m, pollution sources, proposed depth and casing diameter.

WARMA's My-WARMA portal describes water permits for abstraction above 10,000 litres/day for domestic purposes and above 20,000 litres/day for agricultural purposes. Users should verify current rules and their specific circumstances with WARMA; the website must not give legal advice or imply every domestic borehole has the same permit requirements.

The Water Resources Management (Groundwater and Boreholes) Regulations, 2018 (S.I. No. 20 of 2018) are described in FAOLEX as providing for notice of intention to drill, application to WARMA and borehole registration. ZCSA's current public list of compulsory standards includes ZS 190:2010, Drinking Water Quality — Specification (SI 68:2017). Water quality must be tested; clear appearance is not proof of potability.

## 2. Customer types
- **Homeowners and residential estates:** new water source, household use, garden supply, storage and pressure.
- **Farmers and horticulture operators:** irrigation schedules, livestock water, seasonal demand, pump duty, storage and distribution.
- **Schools, clinics and institutions:** multiple points of use, predictable supply, maintainability, records and water-quality assurance.
- **Commercial and industrial sites:** operational continuity, process demand, larger volumes, system monitoring and planned maintenance.
- **Developers and contractors:** site due diligence, borehole planning, coordination with civil works and handover documentation.
- **NGOs and community projects:** documented scope, transparent procurement, field reporting, safe water considerations and long-term maintainability.

## 3. Common services / products
1. Hydrogeological review and geophysical siting investigation.
2. Borehole drilling using a method selected for the site and geology.
3. Casing, screen, gravel pack where specified, sanitary seal and borehole development.
4. Step/constant-rate pumping tests, recovery observations and field reports.
5. Submersible pump, rising main, cabling, controls and protection.
6. Solar or grid-powered pumping options where suitable.
7. Water storage tanks, stands, pipework and reticulation.
8. Existing borehole diagnosis, cleaning/redevelopment and pump replacement.
9. Water sampling coordination and laboratory testing by competent providers.
10. Maintenance inspections and operating guidance.

## 4. Buying motivations
- Reliable access to water for household, agricultural or operational needs.
- A single accountable project scope rather than coordinating multiple vendors.
- Reduced uncertainty before rig mobilisation.
- Evidence of contractor competence and regulatory readiness.
- Clear expectations on what the quote includes, what can change and what is not guaranteed.
- Correctly matched pump, storage and distribution rather than buying components in isolation.
- Records that help with future repairs, water-quality checks and asset management.

## 5. Market conventions observed
Across the reviewed Zambian provider sites, common conventions include: a direct “get a quote” or WhatsApp CTA; a broad list of services; customer segments (home, farm, business, institution); claims about experience, rig capacity or project counts; photographs of rigs and installations; and a short step-by-step process. Many providers package survey, drilling, casing, pumps and tanks as one end-to-end solution.

A more trustworthy concept should avoid unsupported “guaranteed water” claims and instead show a clear project sequence, explain uncertainty, describe the records handed over and identify which tasks depend on qualified third parties or regulatory approval.

## 6. Trust signals
- Verifiable WARMA drilling-company licence and relevant technical credentials.
- Registered/qualified hydrogeology and drilling personnel where required.
- Clear description of rigs, pump-test equipment and technical capability.
- Written quotation with scope, assumptions, exclusions, variation process and payment milestones.
- Site investigation findings and a documented drilling/completion log.
- Test-pumping record with method and observations.
- Pump model, installation details, controls and commissioning checklist.
- Water-quality results from a competent laboratory; never substitute appearance for testing.
- Clear aftercare and workmanship warranty terms.
- Real project evidence with client permission, location context and dates.

The fictional brand deliberately does **not** claim a WARMA licence, NCC/EIZ registration, project count, completed projects or customer ratings.

## 7. Useful website features
- Separate service pages/sections for survey, drilling, pumping, storage, rehabilitation and water-quality coordination.
- A “how it works” process with deliverables at each stage.
- Project cards segmented by residential, agriculture and institutional use.
- A practical pre-drilling checklist and WARMA links.
- A clear caution that a geophysical survey cannot guarantee water or a specific yield.
- Water-quality guidance that says testing is required before drinking.
- A project brief builder that captures location, property type, intended use, approximate daily demand, existing borehole condition, access and service needs.
- FAQ/policy section covering site access, variations, yield uncertainty, permits, water testing, warranty and cancellation.
- Mobile-friendly navigation and click-to-contact details.

### Aquaquest vs drilling contractors
The comparison suggests two adjacent positioning lanes: (1) technical water-resource investigation/supervision and (2) field contractor delivering drilling and pump/tank works. UNDERFLOW is designed as a specialist contractor with a strong documentation and responsible-water message. That positioning is a design choice, not a market-share claim.

## 10. Sourced facts vs design assumptions

### Sourced facts
- WARMA publishes drilling-company licence and borehole-registration information.
- WARMA's published guidance includes a 30 m domestic site-map radius and non-domestic site-investigation details including boreholes within 250 m.
- My-WARMA states abstraction thresholds for permit applications; users should confirm applicability with WARMA.
- FAOLEX identifies the 2018 Groundwater and Boreholes Regulations and describes notice/application/registration requirements.
- ZCSA lists ZS 190:2010 as a compulsory drinking-water quality standard.
- Reviewed local businesses publicly list combinations of groundwater surveys, drilling, test pumping, pumps, tanks, reticulation and water-quality services.

### Design assumptions (fictional)
- UNDERFLOW Waterworks, its Makeni base, service area, team, contact details, catalogue, workflow, service policies, case studies and all testimonials are invented.
- Any service package, deliverable or process described as “UNDERFLOW” is a design proposal for a fictional company, not a claim about a real contractor.
- No prices, project counts, customer ratings, warranties, licences or technical capacity claims are invented as facts.
- The concept studies are not completed jobs and contain no actual yield data.

**Research limitation:** WARMA's permit portal and some legislation pages can be intermittently slow. This report relies on the official WARMA permitting page, the portal's indexed public content, and the FAOLEX record summary; confirm current forms, fees, thresholds and legal wording directly with WARMA before real-world use.
