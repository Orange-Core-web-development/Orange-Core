# Day 8 research — Companion-animal veterinary clinic in Lusaka

**Research snapshot: 22 November 2026.** PAWLINE is fictional. Market observations below are sourced; business details, sample copy and features are design assumptions.

## Niche and customers
A companion-animal clinic serves dog and cat owners across life stages: first-time pet parents, households maintaining preventive care, owners with illness or injury concerns, senior-pet caregivers, and clients needing procedures or diagnostics. Owners typically need reassurance, clear next steps, transparent appointment expectations, records and convenient access to the clinic. A website should avoid making a diagnosis or guaranteeing treatment outcomes.

## Typical services and market conventions
Common clinic service groupings include general consultations, preventive wellness, vaccinations, parasite prevention, diagnostics, dental care, surgery, grooming or pet supplies where offered, and pet travel assistance. Actual service availability and scope vary by licensed facility and staff. Local examples demonstrate mixed-animal care and supplies (Bayhealth), general checkups/vaccinations/emergency and surgery information (Chelston), mobile visits (Katesa), and broad service listings (Francofile).

## Trust signals and useful features
- Show a current Animal Health Facility licence only if real and verified; link to the Veterinary Council of Zambia certificate-verification source.
- Identify registered practitioners truthfully; never fabricate credentials.
- Explain hours, location, appointment expectations, records, pricing process and what to do when a pet needs urgent help.
- Use an accessible appointment enquiry form, new-patient checklist, pet records reminder, FAQs and service categories.
- Make clear that emergency availability is not guaranteed and that urgent symptoms require immediate contact with an actual veterinary provider.

## Regulation and public verification
The Veterinary Council of Zambia (VCZ) registers veterinary professionals and licenses animal-health facilities. The Veterinary and Veterinary Para-Professions Act No. 45 of 2010 governs registration and licensing. VCZ advises public users to check facility licences and practitioner certificates. On 20 November 2026, VCZ published a warning that animal-health facilities must hold a valid 2026 licence; it reiterates licence display and annual renewal requirements.

- Official VCZ homepage and certificate verification: https://member.vaz.org.zm/
- VCZ FAQ, facility licensing and public verification guidance: https://member.vaz.org.zm/faq
- VCZ notice dated 20 Nov 2026: https://member.vaz.org.zm/news/72
- Veterinary and Veterinary Para-Professions Act (PDF hosted by Chikankata Council): https://www.chikankatacouncil.gov.zm/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/THE-VETERINARY-AND-VETERINARY-PARA-PROFESSIONS-ACT-2010-1.pdf

## Fictional brand assumptions
PAWLINE Veterinary Studio is an invented small-animal practice in Kabulonga, Lusaka. Its sample hours, address, contact details, staff bios, prices, testimonial and policy statements are fictional placeholders, not actual services or medical claims. The design intentionally has no fabricated licence number or real phone number. Contact and booking actions only create a copyable local enquiry summary.
