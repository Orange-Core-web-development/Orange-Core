# Day 15 research — Branded packaging, labels and short-run print production in Zambia

**Research date:** 28 November 2026  
**Geographic focus:** Lusaka, with regional delivery considered by arrangement  
**Concept:** FOLDLINE Packaging Studio (fictional)  
**Scope:** Design-led paper packaging, folding cartons, mailer boxes, product sleeves, labels, paper bags, inserts and point-of-sale pieces. This is not a claim to manufacture flexible films, rigid plastic containers or industrial corrugated board at national scale.

## 1. Niche overview
Packaging suppliers in Zambia serve businesses that need to protect, identify, present, store and move products. The public market spans several different production models: large-scale corrugated carton manufacturing; rigid plastic container manufacturing; commercial printers that also offer labels and branded boxes; and smaller design/print providers.

A useful website must make the production scope legible. “Packaging” can mean a stock container, a custom structural box, a printed label, a corrugated shipper or a complete design-to-production service. These are not interchangeable. A good enquiry captures the product, dimensions, weight, quantity, handling conditions, print requirements, deadline and any food-contact or regulatory considerations before suggesting a format.

## 2. Customer types
- Food and beverage makers: cartons, sleeves, labels, takeaway boxes, dividers and shipping cases.
- Beauty and personal-care brands: folding cartons, bottle labels, tamper-evident seals, gift boxes and inserts.
- Agricultural producers and processors: produce cartons, labels, trays, dividers and transport packaging.
- Retail and e-commerce businesses: mailers, branded shipper boxes, tissue, stickers, inserts and paper bags.
- Hospitality and food service: takeaway packaging, menu sleeves, delivery labels, cup carriers and event packaging.
- Manufacturers and distributors: protective cartons, parts boxes, product identification labels and repeat-order consistency.
- Agencies and brand studios: prototypes, presentation mock-ups, short runs, campaign packs and artwork support.

6. Dividers, trays, partitions and product inserts.

## 4. Buying motivations
- Product protection and fit.
- Shelf recognition and readable information.
- Operational fit with filling, sealing, storage, picking and dispatch.
- Predictable repeat orders with stable specifications and artwork versions.
- Appropriate material selection for the product, environment and intended contact conditions.
- Production quantities that suit launches, seasonal tests or established repeat runs.
- Clear proofing and approval before a production run is committed.

## 5. Market conventions and trust signals
Publicly visible Zambian providers describe catalogue products, bespoke manufacture, design/print, artwork preparation, production, finishing and delivery. Common trust signals include a visitable production address, clearly described equipment and scope, sample products, documented specifications, print proofs, repeat-order controls, realistic lead-time guidance, quality procedures and a clear quote process.

A credible site should distinguish what is produced in-house from what is sourced or subcontracted. Explain the approval stage, artwork responsibility, tolerances, colour expectations, tooling charges where applicable, quantity bands, delivery boundaries, and how damaged or non-conforming goods are handled. Avoid unsupported claims such as “food safe”, “compostable”, “recyclable everywhere”, “FSC certified”, “zero waste” or guaranteed turnaround.

## 7. Regulatory and standards considerations
- The Zambia Bureau of Standards (ZABS) currently lists the ZS 033 series for labelling of pre-packaged foods: Part 1 general guidelines, Part 2 date marking, Part 3 nutrition labelling and Part 4 claims. The public catalogue describes the intended scope of each part. Product owners should confirm applicable standards and current requirements with ZABS or the relevant authority.  
  https://www.zabs.org.zm/buy-standards
- The U.S. International Trade Administration's Zambia country guide, last published **23 March 2026**, states that imported food products require English-language labels detailing ingredients and expiration dates, and directs readers to the Zambia Compulsory Standards Agency. This is a specific imported-food summary, not a complete rulebook for every Zambian product category.  
  https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/zambia-labeling-and-marking-requirements
- The Food Safety Act No. 7 of 2019 includes provisions against misleading or deceptive food labelling and packaging. The exact requirements depend on the product and applicable standards; this concept uses a customer-approved artwork workflow and does not offer legal certification.  
  https://www.ubutabera.com/public-laws/zambia/the-food-safety-act-2019

## 8. Useful website features
- Filterable product-format catalogue with clear use cases.
- Material guide distinguishing folding carton board, corrugated board and label stocks.
- Sector pathways that start with the product and its journey.
- Project brief builder capturing dimensions, weight, quantity, print, finish and deadline.
- Artwork checklist explaining dielines, bleed, safe areas, fonts and image resolution.
- Visible proof-approval stage and explicit statement that production starts only after approval.
- Clearly labelled illustrative concept work rather than fabricated client logos, testimonials or production statistics.
- FAQ and policy content covering samples, tolerances, colour, revisions, quantities and delivery.
