# Day 13 research — live event technical production & staging

**Research date:** 27 November 2026  
**Market lens:** Lusaka, Zambia; regional mobilisation by project arrangement  
**Niche boundary:** The technical layer of live events: audio, lighting, staging/rigging, LED/video, temporary power, show control and technical crew. This is distinct from full event planning, décor, catering, artist booking and venue management.

## Niche and customers
Live-event technical production translates an event brief, venue and running order into a safe, intelligible and engaging technical environment. Projects commonly combine equipment hire with design, transport, installation, operation and dismantling. Buyers may need one discipline (such as conference audio) or a coordinated package.

Customer groups include corporate communications and marketing teams (AGMs, launches, awards and town halls); conference organisers and PCOs (multi-session and hybrid programmes); promoters and festival producers; venues and hotels; creative agencies (brand activations and product reveals); couples/private hosts; and NGOs or institutions. Their motivations are reduced event-day uncertainty, intelligible speech, good sightlines, reliable schedules, clear scope, technical coordination and a single accountable contact.

## Common services and products
- **Audio:** PA, subwoofers, monitors, digital mixing, microphones, playback and front-of-house operation.
- **Lighting:** LED wash/profile fixtures, moving lights, control desks, DMX programming and stage/room looks.
- **Stage and rigging:** modular decks, stairs, ramps, truss, ground-support structures, scenic masking and approved suspension systems.
- **Video:** LED surfaces, projection, confidence monitors, cameras, playback and switching.
- **Power:** load schedules, generator interface, distribution, cable routes, protection and backup planning.
- **Show operations:** technical direction, cue sheets, operators, stage-side coordination, rehearsal, show-call and strike.
- **Dry hire:** equipment-only rental may be offered, but compatibility, collection/return, operator responsibility and damage terms should be explicit.

These are category observations from provider service menus, not a claim that every supplier owns every item.

## Buying conventions and trust signals
A useful technical enquiry captures event date/access times; venue and dimensions; indoor/outdoor status; audience and seating format; event type and running order; presenters/performers and microphone needs; screen/content needs; available power; rigging restrictions; and budget range.

Credible trust signals include a named technical contact, site review for complex builds, an equipment/scope schedule, load-in/rehearsal/show-call/strike plan, power and cable-routing plan, outdoor weather contingency, crew roles, written exclusions and overtime/cancellation terms, genuine case studies with permission, and substantiated insurance/training/inspection evidence. Avoid fabricated client logos, inflated audience numbers, “100% safety” claims and unverified inventory counts.

## Useful website features
1. Filterable catalogue for audio, lighting, stage/rigging, video, power and show operations.
2. Event-format routes for corporate, conference, festival, brand activation and private events.
3. Case studies showing brief, venue constraints, response and deliverables; fictional examples must be labelled.
4. Clear pre-production process and information needed before quoting.

6. Site-readiness prompts covering access, audience layout, power, weather, rigging and venue rules.

8. Direct contact paths for real providers; this concept uses non-operational placeholders.

## Safety and regulatory context
The UK Health and Safety Executive (HSE) publishes event guidance on temporary demountable structures, electrical safety and venue/site design. Its electrical guidance highlights site layout, power requirements, generators, earthing, cable routing, access restriction and weather protection. Its site-design guidance covers audience profile, access, emergency plans, capacity, circulation, ground conditions and site plans. These are useful professional references, **not Zambian law or a substitute for competent local engineering advice**:
- Temporary demountable structures: https://www.hse.gov.uk/event-safety/temporary-demountable-structures.htm
- Electrical safety: https://www.hse.gov.uk/event-safety/electrical-safety.htm
- Venue and site design: https://www.hse.gov.uk/event-safety/venue-site-design.htm

The Event Safety Alliance describes its Event Safety Guide as covering emergency planning, crowd management, weather preparedness, communications, venue/site design, rigging and temporary staging. Its standards list includes event rigging and temporary special-event structures. Applicability depends on jurisdiction and scope: https://eventsafetyalliance.org/standards-guidance-2

For Zambia, the eRegistry states that licence/permit requirements depend on business activity and jurisdiction: https://businesslicenses.gov.zm/. Lusaka City Council publishes venue-hire information: https://www.lcc.gov.zm/hire-a-venue/. Organisers should confirm current requirements for the exact activity, location and venue with the relevant council, venue, police and other authorities. This report is not legal advice and does not claim a universal permit checklist.

## Strategic conclusion
A credible website in this niche should sell preparation and coordination rather than spectacle alone. The strongest experience combines a clear technical service map, event-format routing, transparent pre-production steps, realistic fictional case studies, a practical enquiry builder and an explicit reminder that final rigging, electrical, site and permit requirements need competent review and relevant local approvals.
