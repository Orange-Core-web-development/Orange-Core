# Day 16 Research — Freight forwarding, customs coordination & cross-border logistics in Zambia

**Research date:** 28 November 2026  
**Portfolio project:** TALLYWAY Cargo & Trade (fictional)  
**Market lens:** Lusaka-based importers, exporters and regional commercial cargo movements.

## 1. Niche definition
This niche coordinates commercial goods between suppliers, carriers, ports, border points, warehouses and receiving sites. It can include freight forwarding, road/air/ocean transport coordination, customs documentation, clearing-agent coordination, storage, consolidation, project cargo planning and delivery visibility.

The distinction between a freight coordinator/forwarder and a licensed customs clearing agent matters. Zambia Revenue Authority (ZRA) describes the licensing framework for clearing agents and an online appointment process through which importers/exporters appoint agents. TALLYWAY is deliberately framed as a fictional coordination and planning desk; it does **not** claim to be a licensed clearing agent or to lodge customs declarations.

## 2. Customer types
- Importers and distributors bringing stock, equipment, raw materials or spares into Zambia.
- Exporters of agricultural, manufactured or other commercial goods.
- Mining, construction and infrastructure operators moving equipment and project cargo.
- Manufacturers coordinating recurring inputs and outbound finished goods.
- Retailers and wholesalers consolidating mixed consignments.
- NGOs and institutions coordinating programme cargo, where exemptions and permits must be checked carefully.
- Smaller businesses making a first commercial import and needing help understanding the sequence of tasks.

## 3. Common services
Shipment scoping; road, air, ocean and multimodal coordination; customs clearing by a licensed/appointed agent or authorised self-clearer; import/export document readiness; transit and border hand-off coordination; warehousing/cross-docking/consolidation through partners; project cargo planning; last-mile delivery; milestone reporting and shipment close-out.

These are market service categories, not a claim that one operator has every licence, facility, carrier contract or capability.

## 4. Buying motivations
Customers are buying predictability, visibility and reduced avoidable rework as much as transport itself. Common decision factors include clear separation of freight, handling, insurance, duties, taxes and third-party charges; confidence in route and commodity knowledge; document accuracy; carrier/mode options that fit cargo characteristics; updates that distinguish confirmed events from estimates; licensing/authorisation where regulated customs work is offered; and clear scope, claims and escalation procedures.

## 5. Market conventions and Zambia-specific facts

### Sourced facts
- The U.S. International Trade Administration's Zambia Country Commercial Guide, last published **23 March 2026**, says commercial documents such as bills of lading, airway bills and commercial invoices are required for customs clearance; it references Zambia's ASYCUDA platform, ZRA form CE 20 and advance certification for certain product categories. [Trade.gov — Zambia import requirements & documentation](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/zambia-import-requirements-documentation)
- ZRA's importation guidance describes commercial/freighted cargo declarations and supporting items including invoices, airway bills, cargo/road manifests, bills of lading, contracts, certificates and permits for controlled/restricted goods. [ZRA — Importation of goods](https://www.zra.org.zm/importation-of-goods/)
- ZRA's export guidance says exporters need a TPIN and describes supporting documents such as supplier invoices, carrier cargo manifests, certificates of origin where relevant, consignment notes, permits, packing lists and airway bills. [ZRA — Exportation of goods](https://www.zra.org.zm/exportation-of-goods/)
- ZRA explains that clearing agents are licensed and that importers/exporters appoint them through its online process. Check the current portal instructions before an appointment is made. [ZRA — Clearing agents](https://www.zra.org.zm/clearing-agents/) and [ZRA — Appointment of clearing agents](https://www.zra.org.zm/appointment-of-clearing-agents-2/)
- COMESA describes the Regional Customs Transit Guarantee (RCTG-Carnet) as a customs transit regime for goods moving under customs seals in the COMESA region. Its applicability and operational status must be checked for the particular country, route and consignment. [COMESA — Trade facilitation programme activities](https://www.comesa.int/programme-activities-trade-facilitation/)
- ICC's Incoterms® learning material explains that trade terms allocate selected delivery obligations, costs and risks; they should be used with a precise named place and the current ICC rules. [ICC Academy — Incoterms®](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/)

### Practical conventions
A commercial shipment file commonly needs a goods description, commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, origin information and permits/certificates relevant to the product and route. Classification, customs value, origin and product controls can change the paperwork and cost. A quote should separate freight from customs duties/taxes, insurance, port/terminal handling, storage, inspections and local delivery.

No generic clearance time, duty rate, route distance or freight price is published in this concept because these are shipment-specific and change with the commodity, classification, carrier, route, documents and authority decisions.

## 6. Trust signals
- Identify the legal entity and operating address (for a real business).
- Publish current clearing-agent licence details only if licensed and the scope is accurate.
- Explain the client's online agent-appointment responsibility and link to official ZRA guidance.
- Separate owned assets, subcontracted carriers, partner warehouses and planned capabilities.
- Show quote breakdowns and exclusions.
- Explain insurance, cargo liability, claims and proof-of-delivery.
- Use genuine case studies and named references only with permission.
- Provide a documented update cadence and named escalation contact.

## 8. Competitor/reference observations
Public company pages show a multi-service market and several useful website conventions:
- [CMK Logistics Solutions](https://cmklogisticsltd.com/) presents customs clearing, freight transport, warehousing support, supply-chain management, compliance and procurement/documentation support. It distinguishes operational presence from registered/head-office locations and says job status is confirmed by the team rather than claiming live vehicle tracking.
- [Fastline Clearing and Forwarding](https://fastlineclearing.net/) lists customs clearance, air/sea/road freight, local delivery, transit cargo and precious-mineral clearance; its FAQ addresses agent appointment, documents, duty calculation and timing dependencies.
- [Discovery Freight & Logistics](https://discovery.co.zm/index.html) combines customs clearing, forwarding, warehousing, road/rail, air and sea freight, with quote and sector/service navigation.
- [Global Logistics Zambia](https://globalzm.com/freight-forwarding.php) describes air, sea and road freight, warehousing, customs clearing and consolidation/groupage.
- [Qenn Cargo](https://www.qenncargo.com/services) shows a broad service menu including customs clearance, import/export documentation, sea/road/air freight, warehousing, local distribution and consolidation.

**Design opportunity:** Rather than repeat a generic “end-to-end logistics” promise, TALLYWAY is designed around the shipment file: clear hand-offs, document readiness, route assumptions, milestone ownership and transparent limits. This is a design hypothesis from the competitor review, not a measured demand claim.

## 10. Research limitations
This is desk research from public pages, not interviews, customer surveys, tender data or a paid market-size report. Competitor claims have not been independently audited. ZRA requirements can change; verify current instructions for each actual shipment. Corridor names are illustrative route families, not current routing advice or a guarantee of port, border or carrier availability. The website is a fictional portfolio demonstration, not a logistics offer, legal opinion, customs classification, duty assessment or shipment booking.
