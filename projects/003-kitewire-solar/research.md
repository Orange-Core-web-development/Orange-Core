**Project:** Kitewire Solar & Storage

**Status:** Fictional business and portfolio website. No operating company, licence, installer, product stock, customer, project or performance record is represented.

## 1. Niche definition

The concept focuses on a specialist solar and storage integrator serving three overlapping customer groups:

- Households seeking backup for selected essential circuits or a hybrid rooftop PV and battery system.
- Small businesses that need to keep critical operations running through interruptions and want to understand solar, storage and grid interaction.
- Productive-use sites such as workshops, farms and water-pumping installations where equipment duty cycles and site conditions shape the design.

The website is not a generic panel shop or a utility-scale developer site. Its commercial job is to explain the design process, make the service scope understandable, help a visitor prepare a load brief, and convert an initial interest into a properly scoped assessment.

## 2. Market context and dated facts

### Electricity access and system context

The World Bank's 1 April 2025 press release states that electricity access in Zambia had risen from 30% in 2017 to nearly 50%, and notes that hydropower represented about 80% of generation at the time. It discusses solar home systems and mini-grids as part of the country's wider access strategy and identifies technical and financial obstacles to scaling them. These are dated national-level context figures, not a current count of Lusaka customers or an estimate of this fictional business's market size.

Source: https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/04/01/solar-mini-grids-and-off-grid-systems-could-bring-electricity-to-8-5-million-afe-zambians-by-2030

### Net metering

ZESCO announced that its net-metering programme commenced on 1 August 2024. Its public portal provides an application route and lists the information applicants should prepare, including a ZESCO service or meter number, identity or incorporation documents, TPIN, landlord consent where relevant, and property landmark or GPS coordinates.

Sources:
- https://www.zesco.co.zm/fqms.php
- https://netmetering.zesco.co.zm/

The Energy Regulation Board's press statement dated 31 December 2025 sets the 2026 reference net-metering tariff at US$c6.00/kWh (K1.350/kWh) and says the ZESCO Distribution Use of System charge for 2026 shall not exceed US$c3.00/kWh (K0.66/kWh). These are dated regulatory reference values, not a promise of bill savings, a retail electricity tariff, or a payback calculation. They should be rechecked against the regulator and utility before being used in a live commercial proposal.

Source: https://www.erb.org.zm/approved-reference-tariffs-for-net-metering-programme-and-the-respective-connection-fees/

### Regulation and competence

The ERB's renewable-energy guidance describes regulatory steps and technical requirements for electricity-generation projects, while the Zambia Off-Grid Information Hub explains that manufacturing, wholesaling, importing and installing solar energy systems are regulated activities and require the appropriate rights/licence before the relevant business activity begins. The Hub also discusses equipment standards and the role of competent technical personnel.

Sources:
- https://www.erb.org.zm/renewable/
- https://offgrid.gov.zm/en/Licensing
- https://offgrid.gov.zm/en/FAQ
- https://portal.erb.org.zm/information_hub/

The exact authorisation and approval pathway depends on the actual activity, project size, grid connection and applicable rules. A website should not collapse all solar work into one generic licence claim. For a real business, check current ERB requirements, applicable Zambian standards, Engineering Institution of Zambia registration where relevant, ZEMA or local approvals where relevant, and ZESCO's current interconnection process.

## 3. Customer groups and buying motivations

### Homeowners and landlords
- Want selected essentials such as refrigeration, lights, connectivity and security to remain available.
- Need clarity about which circuits will be backed up and for how long under stated assumptions.
- May want to reduce grid dependence, but should not be sold a guaranteed savings or payback figure without verified bills, tariff assumptions and site data.
- Need an understandable explanation of battery capacity, inverter limits, system expansion and maintenance.

### Small businesses and offices
- Need continuity for point-of-sale, connectivity, security, workstations, lighting, refrigeration or selected equipment.
- Care about downtime, opening hours, load priorities and future expansion.
- Need a clear component schedule, scope, exclusions, warranty route and service process.
- May need an assessment of whether critical loads should be separated from non-critical circuits.

### Farms, workshops and productive-use sites
- Need systems designed around duty cycles, motor start-up, pump head, flow, water storage, seasonal use, remote location and maintenance access.
- Often need a site-specific feasibility discussion rather than a pre-packed residential kit.
- Need to understand what happens during cloudy periods, extended demand and equipment faults.

These are customer and buying-motivation hypotheses for the website concept, informed by the observed service models and technical context. They are not findings from a commissioned customer survey.

## 4. Common products and services

Across the reviewed Zambian provider websites, the visible offer set commonly includes:
- PV modules and roof or ground mounting.
- Hybrid or off-grid inverters.
- Lithium battery storage.
- Complete system packages and component sales.
- Residential backup and hybrid solar systems.
- Commercial and institutional installations.
- Load assessments, system sizing and quotation requests.
- Solar pumping and other productive-use applications.
- Cleaning, maintenance, repairs, audits and technical support.
- Product delivery or collection, where offered.

A real quote should distinguish equipment supply from installation, protection accessories, cabling, mounting, testing, commissioning, transport, taxes, utility applications and after-sales support.

## 6. Trust signals and conversion features

For a real solar installation business, useful trust signals would include:
- A verifiable ERB authorisation appropriate to the activities offered.
- Named technical personnel and verifiable professional registration or qualifications where required.
- Actual site photographs and project records with customer permission.
- Transparent system schedules, component datasheets and warranty documents.
- Written scope, assumptions, exclusions and commissioning/testing records.
- A clear service and maintenance route after handover.
- Honest explanation of battery runtime, inverter surge limits, backup circuits and system expansion.
- A documented process for grid connection and net-metering applications where applicable.
- Realistic explanations of savings and payback based on the customer's actual bills and operating profile.

Useful website features include separate home/business/productive-use service pages, a load-planning tool, a transparent process page, technical FAQs, a quote brief builder, and a clearly explained contact path.

## 8. Website plan and interactions

Five independent static pages:
1. **Home:** brand story, customer pathways, process overview and CTA.
2. **Systems:** residential, commercial and productive-use service concepts, component explanations and scope boundaries.
3. **How we work:** discovery, site/load review, design, installation/handover and questions to ask any installer.
4. **Load planner:** editable sample appliance list, daily energy estimate, connected-load estimate, illustrative battery planning figure and copyable snapshot.
5. **Contact:** fictional business details, service-area note and locally generated project brief.

The load planner uses user-adjustable example wattages and hours. Its illustrative battery calculation assumes selected daily energy is spread evenly over 24 hours, an entered backup window, 80% usable battery energy and a 20% planning allowance. The connected-load figure assumes all selected appliances run simultaneously and applies a simple 25% headroom factor to produce an inverter conversation figure. These assumptions are not engineering standards or a system recommendation. The tool does not model motor starting current, actual load profiles, inverter efficiency, solar resource, battery chemistry, temperature, wiring, protection, roof structure or grid approval.

The contact form only validates and prepares copyable text in the browser. It does not transmit or store the visitor's answers.

## 10. Commercial-readiness checklist

Before adapting the concept for a real provider, verify:
- Business registration, trading address, contact channels and service territory.
- Current ERB licence or authorisation for the exact activities undertaken.
- Competence, registration and appointment of responsible technical personnel where required.
- Applicable Zambian standards, equipment conformity and inverter compatibility.
- Site-specific roof/ground, shading, cable-route, distribution-board, earthing and structural conditions.
- ZESCO approval and net-metering eligibility where grid export is proposed.
- Real product brands, datasheets, stock, pricing, warranty terms and service availability.
- Tax treatment, payment terms, delivery, cancellation, privacy and consumer requirements.
- Any claims about savings, payback, uptime, system performance or customer results, backed by appropriate evidence and disclosed assumptions.
