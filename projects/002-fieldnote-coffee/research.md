# Day 02 research — Specialty coffee roasting and direct-to-consumer coffee

**Project:** Fieldnote Roasters  
**Research date:** 16 November 2026  
**Concept market:** Cape Town, South Africa  
**Status:** Fictional business and website concept. No trading business, producer relationship, stock, service or customer evidence is claimed.

## 1. Niche definition

The project focuses on an independent micro-roastery selling roasted coffee directly to home drinkers while also presenting a wholesale proposition for cafés, offices and hospitality venues. It combines four connected website jobs:

1. Explain the roaster's point of view and how it selects and presents coffees.
2. Help a home brewer choose a bag by brew method, flavour preference and product detail.
3. Encourage repeat purchases through a simple subscription/club proposition.
4. Give trade customers a clear route to ask about supply, calibration and support.

This is not a general café directory or a restaurant website. The fictional business is a small roastery brand with an online catalogue and a trade desk.

## 2. Customer groups and buying motivations

### Home brewers
- Want coffee that suits their grinder, brewer and taste.
- Need practical information: whole bean or grind choice, roast style, bag size, tasting descriptors, origin and brew suggestions.
- May be curious about trying new origins but uncertain which bag to buy.
- Value freshness, useful guidance, clear pricing and delivery/collection information.

### Enthusiasts and gift buyers
- Look for distinct origins, processing information, tasting notes, limited releases and a sense of discovery.
- Benefit from sample formats, curated boxes, gift notes and accessible brew education.
- Need product information that is specific without pretending flavour descriptors are objective guarantees.

### Cafés, restaurants, offices and hospitality
- Need consistent supply, predictable ordering, fit with equipment and menu, and a supplier who can help staff dial in recipes.
- Often need wholesale terms, delivery zones, minimum order information, training/support boundaries and a named contact.
- Need a credible trade enquiry rather than consumer-style checkout copy.

These customer motivations are design hypotheses informed by the product and service patterns visible on the referenced roaster and retailer websites. They are not the result of a commissioned survey.

## 3. Common products and services observed

The researched businesses show a broad but recognisable offer set:
- 250 g and larger retail bags; whole-bean and selected grind options.
- House blends and rotating single-origin coffees.
- One-off purchases and recurring subscriptions.
- Wholesale coffee for cafés, offices, churches or hospitality.
- Brew equipment and accessories through specialist retailers.
- Coffee education, barista training, calibration or event service in some business models.
- Discovery/sample boxes as a lower-commitment way to try several roasters or coffees.

Examples:
- Apothecary Coffee describes small-batch roasting, roast-date communication, retail bags, a subscription, wholesale supply and an event coffee trailer.
- Trade Route Coffees presents small-batch Cape Town roasting, countrywide delivery, local collection, retail bags, equipment, capsules, wholesale and event-trailer services.
- Yellow Jacket Coffee explains roast/rest timing, grind selection and wholesale applications.
- SMPL Coffee uses a discovery-box model with small samples from independent roasters.
- Cape Coffee Beans combines roasted coffee from multiple roasters with machines, grinders and accessories.

## 4. Buying motivations and useful trust signals

Useful trust signals for this niche include:
- A clear roast or dispatch rhythm, stated only when operationally true.
- Product pages with origin, process, roast approach, flavour descriptors, bag size and brew suggestions.
- A transparent explanation of what a subscription includes, when it renews and how it can be paused or cancelled.
- Clear delivery, collection, returns and customer-service information.
- Real photographs of the roastery, packaging and people, with permission and accurate captions.
- Verifiable producer relationships and certifications only when documented.
- Trade information that addresses consistency, volume, equipment and staff support.
- Brewing guidance that helps customers use the product successfully.

The Specialty Coffee Association's Coffee Value Assessment is useful as a framework for thinking about coffee value through physical, descriptive, affective and extrinsic attributes. It reinforces the idea that flavour language is only one part of a fuller coffee story. The SCA Coffee Skills Program identifies practical learning areas including barista skills, roasting, brewing, sensory evaluation, green coffee and equipment maintenance.

The fictional brand intentionally makes no certification, farm-direct, ethical-certification, sustainability or producer-partnership claims.

## 5. Market conventions and reference observations

| Reference | Observed pattern | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| Apothecary Coffee, Cape Town | Retail bags, roast-date/freshness messaging, subscriptions, wholesale and event trailer | Make direct-to-consumer and trade routes visible; give recurring coffee a distinct explanation |
| Trade Route Coffees, Cape Town | Small-batch roasting, delivery and collection, multiple product formats, wholesale and event service | Explain how customers receive coffee and what formats exist |
| Yellow Jacket Coffee, Cape Town | Resting/roast explanation, grind choices, FAQ and wholesale application | Put practical brew and freshness guidance near product decisions |
| SMPL Coffee | Discovery box with several small samples from independent roasters | Make discovery and low-commitment trial part of the concept |
| Cape Coffee Beans | Multi-roaster retail alongside equipment and accessories | Keep Fieldnote's own offer focused rather than imitating a large equipment catalogue |

These are observations of public-facing website propositions, not independent verification of the companies' operational performance, customer satisfaction or commercial results. No competitor text, logos, photography or product names are reused.

## 8. Website feature plan

- Five pages: home, coffees, brew notes, wholesale and contact.
- Product cards with category filters and add-to-bag interactions.
- Local-only sample bag stored in browser localStorage; users can remove items and copy an illustrative order note.
- Coffee finder that recommends a product based on brew method and flavour preference.
- Brew-ratio calculator with selectable baseline ratios and method shortcuts.
- Wholesale and contact enquiry builders that validate fields and generate copyable text locally.
- Clear sample pricing, concept-only fulfilment and fictional-contact notices.
- Responsive navigation, keyboard-operable controls, visible focus styles and reduced-motion support.

## 9. Design assumptions and boundaries

The choice of Cape Town, the Salt River concept location, business name, product lineup, bag prices, opening hours, contact details, monthly club and proposed service tiers are creative assumptions. They are not research findings.

The design uses an editorial paper/ink palette with orange, cobalt, lime and lilac product identities. All SVG illustrations are original project artwork. No external image, font, icon, model, sound or JavaScript library is loaded.

The bag is a visual selection tool only. There is no payment gateway, checkout, order API, email submission, analytics, mailing-list capture or customer database. Enquiry builders operate locally in the browser and do not send the entered information anywhere.

## 10. Commercial-readiness gaps

Before adapting this concept for a real roaster, replace all fictional details and verify:
- Business registration, address, trading hours and contact details.
- Actual coffee availability, origins, producer relationships and lot data.
- Product weights, grind options, roast dates and packaging information.
- Pricing, tax treatment, shipping, collection, returns and cancellation terms.
- Subscription renewal, payment, pause and cancellation logic.
- Wholesale capacity, minimums, delivery routes and service commitments.
- Any food labelling, consumer, privacy and e-commerce obligations applicable to the actual business and sales channels.
- Permission for all customer testimonials, photography and brand assets.
